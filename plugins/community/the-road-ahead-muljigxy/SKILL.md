# PART 2: SPEAKER SCRIPT

*Delivery notes: Speak at a calm, deliberate pace — roughly 130 words per minute. Pause markers [pause] indicate 2–3 seconds of silence. Total spoken time: approximately 12 minutes.*

## Slide 1 — Cover

Good afternoon, everyone. Thank you for being here.

Most of you have seen the agents we are already building and delivering for clients. Today, on behalf of our leadership, I want to tell you what comes next.

Leadership has decided that agent development is no longer something a few teams work on. It becomes a direction for the whole company. This is not another session about using AI to work faster — we have had those. Today is about the work we do for clients, and what changes for each of us.

And this concerns every team — engineering, DevOps, QA, project management, design, HR and operations, communications, finance.

[pause]

---

## Slide 2 — The decision

So what actually changes?

Until now, agent development has sat with a few projects and a few teams. You have seen the first products being built and delivered.

From now on, it is a company-wide direction — with resources behind it. More resources are being aligned to this work, every department has a part in it, and we will run it one deliberate way, together.

What does not change: our full commitment to our current projects and clients. This expands what we do — it does not replace it.

[pause]

---

## Slide 3 — Why now

Why now? Because our clients are asking — and the wider market says the same.

KPMG's latest AI Pulse survey, published just this week, found that sixty-two percent of large enterprises are now building, deploying or developing AI agents. That is up from fifty-three percent only one quarter earlier. And a quarter of them are already working on multi-agent systems — up from six percent.

But building is not the same as delivering. Deloitte found that only eleven percent of organizations have agentic systems actually running in production.

Many are building agents. Few have them in production. That gap is exactly where clients need a partner who can build these systems properly. That is the role we are already playing — and now, as a whole company.

[pause]

---

## Slide 4 — The work

So what are clients actually asking for? Across the industry, five architecture patterns dominate: retrieval-augmented pipelines, single-agent workflows, multi-agent orchestration, human-in-the-loop systems, and AI pipelines.

These are the shapes of the work in front of us.

---

## Slide 5 — Why we're ready

Why are we ready for this? Because an AI agent is a system — not just a model.

It has five parts. The **model** is the reasoning engine. **Tools** — the APIs, databases and services it acts through — are integration work, our engineering bread and butter. **Context and memory** need clean data and well-defined workflows — the territory of our analysts and project managers. **Orchestration** runs on infrastructure our DevOps team manages. And **guardrails** — permissions, validation, human approval points — are owned largely by QA and design.

The model is the smallest part. Everything around it is work this company already does.

And agents are built on top of the data, APIs and infrastructure our teams build today — connected through the Model Context Protocol, the open standard now governed by the Linux Foundation. The systems we build are not becoming obsolete. They are the foundation.

[pause]

---

## Slide 6 — Delivery teams

Now — what does this decision change for each team? Let me go team by team.

**Developers and architects.** Your API design, integration, data modeling, and cloud skills carry straight into agent projects. On those projects you will also own context engineering, agent evaluation, and observability for AI systems.

**DevOps.** Your CI/CD, infrastructure, monitoring, and reliability work stays central. On agent projects: deploying and versioning agent systems, tracing agent behavior in production, managing model costs, and securing what agents are permitted to do. Agentic systems are operationally heavier than traditional applications. Our DevOps capability is a competitive advantage here — not a support function.

**QA.** Unlike traditional software, agents do not behave identically every time. Quality moves from "does it pass the test" to "is it accurate, consistent, and safe across many scenarios." A deterministic app fails loudly. An agent fails plausibly. Catching that is a skill the whole industry is short on — and it makes QA more critical on these projects, not less.

**Project managers and business analysts.** Agent projects succeed or fail on process definition: what the agent does, where humans approve, and what "done correctly" means. That is your work at its core.

**UI/UX and design.** Conversational flows, approval screens, transparency cues — an open frontier with very few established conventions. Our designers get to help define them.

[pause]

---

## Slide 7 — Beyond delivery

And beyond the delivery teams — because this is a company decision, not only an engineering one.

**HR and operations.** Building capability for client agent work at this scale is an HR program, not a side activity — and this team runs it. The transition we are describing today is, in large part, a program HR and operations will lead.

**Communications.** How this direction is explained — to our own people, to clients, and externally — shapes whether it lands as opportunity or confusion. Internally, this team keeps everyone informed as it rolls out. Externally, they position our capabilities credibly, without hype. As generated content floods the market, their judgement about tone and accuracy becomes more valuable, not less.

[pause]

---

## Slide 8 — How we'll run it

Now, how we will run this.

I will admit — the pace of change in AI is overwhelming for me as well. New models, new frameworks, new announcements every week. If you feel that, you are not behind. And it is exactly why we will be deliberate.

Gartner expects over forty percent of agentic AI projects to be canceled by the end of 2027 — due to unclear value and insufficient controls. We do not intend to be part of that number.

So three principles: we take on agent projects where the value is clear and the controls are in place. We prioritize reliability over novelty. And we build once and reuse across projects — every agent we deliver should make the next one faster and safer.

Practically: we will shortlist a small number of agent frameworks — on production readiness, integration support including MCP, observability, and community maturity. From the agents we are already building and delivering, we will establish reference architectures and reusable components. And we will expand the portfolio progressively, as our delivery experience grows. Depth before breadth.

[pause]

---

## Slide 9 — What happens next

So what happens next.

More resources are being aligned to this direction — toward agent development and the client proposals in front of us.

We will run a capability program, with structured learning paths open to every department. We will share reference architectures and lessons from our proposals and projects across teams. And there will be pathways to contribute to agent development initiatives alongside your current responsibilities.

Each department lead will follow up with specifics for their team in the coming weeks.

[pause]

---

## Slide 10 — Closing

Let me close with this.

The direction is decided, and the pace will be deliberate. The work every team does today is the foundation of what comes next.

From using AI to building it — we will build the next chapter together.

Thank you.
